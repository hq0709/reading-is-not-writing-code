# Research plan

## Question and registered claim

Do medical vision-language models use the clinical information that can be decoded from their internal representations?

Primary falsifiable claim: under a capacity-controlled, patient-split evaluation, robust linear decodability at a consumed visual locus does not by itself establish direction-specific causal influence under probe-normal interventions.

Falsification: the claim is rejected if prospectively registered cells with positive controlled-decoding intervals also show concept-direction effects that exceed the same-alpha random and sham controls and the uncertainty bounds of every fixed unrelated clinical direction, replicated across the registered architecture/concept contrasts. A true negative is retained as `OBSERVED`; metrics, doses, loci, control identities, or direction-specificity rules are not changed after seeing results to rescue the headline.

## Method identity and forbidden rescues

The method identity is fixed: patient-level splits; the same capacity-matched linear readout across loci; repeated type-to-random-label controls; trained-versus-random initialisation floors; explicit nuisance probes; bidirectional, equal-norm intervention controls; preregistered dose scale; bootstrap uncertainty; and immutable run evidence. Forbidden rescues include replacing AUROC/selectivity after inspection, removing failed cells, changing prompts only for unfavourable concepts without a registered sensitivity analysis, adding a stronger decoder, changing the intervention vector family, or relabelling invalid runs as scientific negatives.

## Current evidence boundary

The repository already contains released observations for controlled decodability, utilisation, planted-ground-truth tests, direction mismatch, and an ISIC second modality. These remain governed by `docs/02_RESULTS.md` and `docs/10_RESULTS.md`. They are not reclassified here. `docs/10_RESULTS.md` also records invalid or incomplete intervention evidence, including an off-forward-path LLaVA `vis.last` hook, incorrect dose scaling, missing repeated control draws, and absent bootstrap intervals.

The evaluation cohort contains exactly 400 test rows from 400 patients absent from both earlier Qwen intervention cohorts. Selection reads no labels or images: exclude every patient represented in the accepted 200-row source cohort and 400-row direction-specificity cohort; choose one row per remaining patient by SHA-256 of `qwen-causal-ownership-row-v1\0<row_id>`; rank patients by SHA-256 of `qwen-causal-ownership-patient-v1\0<patient_id>`; and take the first 400. The canonical JSON row-ID list has SHA-256 `e657a9bd1707f13695b02a341047af6f53981295b840376eb0ea5bbebfe2d312`.

For question `c`, the clinical ownership statistic is

`O_c = Delta(c -> c) - max_{d != c} Delta(d -> c)`,

where `Delta(d -> c)` is the paired mean change in `P(yes)` caused by direction `d`. Uncertainty uses 5,000 joint patient bootstrap draws with seed `20260904`. The formal ownership test is implemented as all 30 fixed pairwise differences `Delta(c -> c) - Delta(d -> c)`: a studentized centered max-T critical value provides simultaneous 95% lower bounds over all 30 comparisons. A concept is owned when all five simultaneous bounds for its column are positive, its diagonal effect is positive, exceeds every one of the 119 same-column random directions, and exceeds the absolute sham effect. Beating all 119 random directions gives the minimum exact one-sided rank p-value `1/120`; Bonferroni over six columns controls this random-control family at 0.05.

For each fixed direction, the shared-alias analysis measures both its mean off-diagonal excess over the other five named diagonals and its mean absolute off-diagonal effect. A studentized centered max-T critical value provides simultaneous 95% lower bounds over all 12 statistics. A shared causal alias requires both bounds to be positive, and its absolute off-diagonal effect must exceed the global maximum across all registered random-direction summaries and the global maximum absolute sham effect. Direction selection occurs only among directions meeting that conjunction. The full matrix, all simultaneous bounds, random/sham comparisons, and bootstrap draws are reported regardless of disposition; valid outcomes are `OBSERVED` and implementation or measurement invalidity is `FAILED`.

The matrix decides the next mechanism gate without another route-selection round. A detected shared alias has first priority and locks the largest raw off-diagonal effect in its confirmed direction for output-Jacobian alignment, answer-encoding replacement, and projection-removal intervention. If no shared alias is detected, diagonal ownership locks the owned concept with the largest simultaneous 95% lower ownership bound for input-induced displacement closure and the same read/write factorisation. Exact ties in either branch are resolved by the registered concept order: Effusion, Atelectasis, Pneumothorax, Cardiomegaly, Mass, then Nodule. If neither confirmatory condition holds, the project switches to a separately registered Qwen Consolidation input-closure gate on a fresh cohort. The immutable runner validates the complete summary and writes this decision to `artifacts/mechanism-route.json`; invalid or internally inconsistent summaries fail closed. Any visual block pattern in this matrix is exploratory and can motivate only a separately registered confirmation. Only the selected confirmatory structure is replicated once on a second architecture. Prompts, concepts, dose, cohort, and statistics are not changed after observing the matrix.

## ICLR evidence expansion

The active research objective is to establish when a readable clinical direction supports a semantic intervention. The sequence separates answer encoding, prompt-conditioned clinical specificity, input-supported intervention geometry, and architecture transfer. Each comparison retains the accepted observations and adds a separately identified experiment. The project task owns research selection, execution and delivery coordination; the independent paper task receives accepted findings and owns manuscript integration.

1. **Answer-encoding crossover.** Does a clinical-direction effect follow the clinical meaning or the answer symbol when the mapping between them changes? The accepted findings are in `docs/ANSWER_ENCODING_RESULTS.md`.
2. **Mass prompt-conditioned specificity.** Does the discovered Mass clinical advantage reproduce on independent patients under the two exchanged A/B mappings, and across matched question wordings? Prepare a fixed-dose confirmation using the same clinical directions, an independent patient cohort and prospectively specified random/sham controls. The paired yes/no comparison separates question wording from response instructions. Lock the cohort, control family, capability criteria and joint statistical decision before collecting outcomes. Use the confirmation to choose the concept and prompt for the geometry gate.
3. **Input-supported intervention geometry.** On matched within-patient studies, how much behavioral change is recovered by a natural activation displacement, its probe component, and its complementary component? Establish a paired behavioral opportunity first; preserve co-occurring-label and acquisition-setting balance, and compare matched perturbation magnitudes. Full donor-token replacement is a measurement positive control. Partial recovery and complementary-component effects test the geometry of causal influence. The detailed protocol is locked before new outcomes are inspected.
4. **Architecture transfer.** Replicate the clinically oriented direction-by-question comparisons in LLaVA using the existing consumed-locus activations and fixed-capacity readout. The cross-model unit is an eligible concept-by-encoding comparison under the same controls, rather than a raw probability-effect ranking. A medical-domain model is a subsequent scope extension if an accessible, verified asset is available.

The first analysis also uses the existing ownership per-patient records to report probability versus logit-margin effects, baseline and steered discrimination, label-conditioned responses, and matrix interaction structure. These are descriptive analyses of existing evidence. They identify measurement or correlation explanations for the new experiments and do not replace earlier gate decisions.

### Qwen answer-encoding crossover

The gate `qwen7b-vislast-answer-encoding` holds the Qwen revision, 336-by-336 processor resolution, consumed `model.visual.blocks.31` locus, six capacity-matched clinical normals, and relative-token intervention fixed. It takes the first 200 rows, in their stored order, from accepted ownership run `20260904T125710Z-a3bd883540eb-causal-ownership` for the intervention comparisons. The last 200 stored rows provide a patient-disjoint capability-calibration cohort, evaluated only at the clean A/B baselines. Each row belongs to a distinct patient. This is a paired sensitivity experiment on an existing cohort; it is not an independent-population replication.

For each of the six concepts in the accepted order, use three encodings. The standard encoding retains the accepted yes/no prompt. The two code prompts are:

```text
Does this chest radiograph show {finding}? Answer A if the finding is present and B if it is absent. Reply with A or B only.
Does this chest radiograph show {finding}? Answer B if the finding is present and A if it is absent. Reply with A or B only.
```

The fixed findings are `a pleural effusion`, `atelectasis`, `a pneumothorax`, `cardiomegaly`, `a lung mass`, and `a lung nodule`. For A/B, take the maximum logit over each letter's available single-token plain and leading-space spellings. The two token sets must be nonempty and disjoint. The primary score is the finding-present minus finding-absent logit margin. The raw A-minus-B margin is also retained, and the secondary probability is the sigmoid of the clinically oriented margin. The standard encoding uses the accepted yes/no scoring implementation.

Each question and encoding receives one unsteered baseline and 27 directions at alpha `-0.25` and `+0.25`: six clinical normals, 20 seed-0 isotropic random directions shared across questions and encodings, and a coordinate-permutation sham of that question's normal. All 27 directions are unit-normalized before the same relative-token alpha scaling. The sham permutation follows the 20 random draws. This produces 55 configurations per question/encoding and 198,000 intervention-cohort image-condition outcomes. The independent calibration baselines add 2,400 outcomes, for 200,400 scientific outcomes in total. All 18 intervention question/encoding blocks and all 12 calibration blocks are reported.

Execution order is fixed: the twelve calibration blocks run first (A-present then B-present, with the six accepted concepts in order), followed by the eighteen intervention blocks (standard, A-present, B-present, with the same concept order). Each intervention block iterates baseline first, then the fixed clinical/random/sham direction order, with negative dose before positive dose within each direction. A primary endpoint requires every calibration and intervention block to be complete. Incomplete runs remain `BLOCKED` for inference and preserve their partial outputs; a completed subset never defines a new primary question set.

Before evaluating either test cohort, a preflight checks the accepted forward-path module, an exact alpha-zero no-op and a nonzero downstream-logit response on a validation image, the A/B token identities, and eight unambiguous text-only presence/absence statements under the two code mappings. Every text-only semantic margin must have the correct sign. A timed pilot uses 64 batches of 16 validation images (1,024 image-condition equivalents), including preprocessing and scoring, with fixed A/B mappings and registered doses. Extrapolated time for 200,400 scientific outcomes must be at most 3.75 hours, leaving time for loading and analysis inside the four-hour run budget. A failed validity or throughput preflight makes the measurement gate `BLOCKED` before the full experiment.

For each question, assess baseline clinical discrimination separately under both A/B encodings on the independent 200-patient calibration cohort. A question is eligible for the primary interpretation if each encoding has at least ten positive and ten negative patients, at least 1,900 valid draws out of 2,000 patient bootstrap samples (seed `20260908`), and a one-sided 95% AUROC lower bound above 0.5. Eligibility is fixed from patients absent from the intervention cohort. Record all questions, their counts, baseline Brier scores, encoding-to-encoding baseline rank correlations, and eligibility. Intervention-cohort baseline metrics remain descriptive. If no question is eligible, the primary comparison is inconclusive.

For a patient, direction, and question at alpha `+0.25`, let `a` and `b` be the raw A-minus-B intervention changes under the A-present and B-present mappings respectively. Define the mapping-odd component `s=(a-b)/2` and the mapping-even component `t=(a+b)/2`. A meaning-following intervention with matched magnitudes reverses its raw direction under the mapping exchange and contributes to `s`; a literal-symbol-following intervention contributes to `t`. Unequal response magnitudes across encodings can also contribute to `t`, so the components are interpreted with the baseline asymmetry and matched-control results.

For a scalar patient response `x`, estimate squared population mean with `U(x)=n/(n-1) * (mean(x)^2 - sum_i(x_i^2)/n^2)`. The primary endpoint is the mean over the six clinical directions of the sum over baseline-eligible questions of `U(t)-U(s)`. This removes the finite-sample covariance contribution to the squared sample-mean contrast. Joint patient bootstrap draws (2,000, seed `20260907`) apply the same estimator to each resampled multiset and give a two-sided percentile 95% interval. The selected question set is fixed independently of these intervention patients, and it is one aggregate endpoint.

For each of the 20 random directions and the question-specific sham, compute the identical per-direction normalized endpoint and interval. A positive clinical interval is interpreted as encoding-even response energy beyond generic perturbations only when the clinical estimate also exceeds every random estimate and the absolute sham estimate. Baseline mean raw A-minus-B margins under both mappings expose response asymmetry. The naive sample-mean energy, all-question contrast, each component matrix, clinically oriented ownership margins, negative-dose response, and baseline-to-steered AUROC/Brier changes remain descriptive. Encoding-even energy alone does not identify a literal-token mechanism or concept-specific ownership; nonsignificance is not evidence of encoding invariance.

For corresponding token arrays `H-` and `H+` at the consumed final visual block, define `D = H+ - H-`, with `T` tokens, and let `u` be the accepted unit clinical probe normal. The mean-pool reader gives `g = <mean_t D_t, u>`. Two decompositions answer distinct questions:

- **Pooled-reader decomposition:** `D_reader,t = g u` and `D_complement = D - D_reader`. Under the flattened Euclidean inner product, this is the orthogonal projection onto the single direction formed by repeating `u` over all `T` tokens. Its complementary component has zero pooled probe displacement.
- **Relative-token write decomposition:** with recipient norms `n_t = ||H-_t||`, let `a = g / mean_t n_t` and `D_write,t = a n_t u`. Its residual `D - D_write` also has zero pooled probe displacement. This is the implementation's norm-weighted realization of the same reader displacement; its residual is generally not Euclidean-orthogonal to the write. A dose cap replaces `a` by its clipped value and generally changes the achieved reader displacement.

These identities are grounded in `ActivationCache._pool` in `src/loci.py`, `Steerer._delta` in `src/intervene.py`, and the displacement-matched closure implementation. Tokenwise projection, `<D_t,u>u` at each token, is a different, `T`-dimensional subspace operation from the pooled-reader projection. A registered comparison must name the operation it implements.

Full donor-token replacement supplies a positive measurement control after token count, order, grid and downstream image-context compatibility are verified. Reader-component, complementary-component and norm-weighted-write interventions use the same recipient baseline. Preserve each decomposition's native component magnitudes; report their norms and achieved reader displacements, with any equal-norm controls defined separately. Nonlinear downstream responses require reporting the interaction between component effects rather than assuming their behavioral recoveries add. Validation-only tests establish tensor reconstruction, pooled-score identities, zero-dose behavior, clipping behavior and downstream consumption before patient outcomes.
