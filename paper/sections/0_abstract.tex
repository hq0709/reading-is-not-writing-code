A linear probe can reveal a clinical label in a representation without showing that the decoded direction selectively affects a model's answer. We test this distinction at the exact final visual block consumed downstream by LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Our registered protocol combines a fixed 512-dimensional linear readout, patient-disjoint evaluation, 20 type-to-random-label controls, patient bootstrap uncertainty, and equal-norm probe-normal interventions against random, sham, and fixed clinical directions. Across three model--concept cells on NIH ChestX-ray14, controlled decoding is robust: AUROC ranges from 0.7742 to 0.8009, and every selectivity interval excludes zero. Neither LLaVA Effusion nor LLaVA Edema clears the registered random/sham intervention criterion. Qwen Effusion clears random and sham in the original cohort and again on 400 independent patients, where it changes mean $P(yes)$ by 0.1945. Yet Nodule steering changes the same answer by 0.2519. The familywise Effusion-minus-maximum-unrelated margin is $-0.0573$ (95\% patient-bootstrap CI $[-0.0694,-0.0450]$). Thus, in these registered cells, robust linear availability at a consumed visual locus does not establish direction-specific causal influence under probe-normal intervention. The results separate three evidence levels for medical vision-language model interpretation: decodability, generic perturbation selectivity, and clinical direction specificity.
